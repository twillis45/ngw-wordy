Run against the **production export**, not the dev server, per the wing's own

|---|---|---|

Both findings are now fixed. Logic was the lowest at review, then Engagement

## Logic — 6 at review, 8 after the fix

**The rank ladder cannot measure what it claims to.** Eight rungs are defined
as percentages of a board's total, and one word dominates that total: the base
is six letters and takes the all-wheel bonus, so it is worth **12 points on a
board whose whole grid is 23–33**. Measured across the warm-up and two themed
boards, the base alone is **36–52% of everything available.**

The play-through shows what that does. Six words, in order, produced:

```
cry    1/23   Novice
fat    2/23   Solid
fry    3/23   Solid
cart   7/23   Clever
tray  11/23   Fluent
crafty 23/23  Complete
```

Six of eight ranks in six words, and the last word crossed **four rungs at
once** — Fluent to Complete. A ladder a single move can leap most of is not
reporting progress, it is reporting whether you have found the long word yet.
The names promise a skill read ("Clever", "Wordsmith") the arithmetic cannot
support.

This is worth stating precisely because the codebase has already fought over
this number once: `rankFor` was moved off `maxScore` and onto the six rows
specifically so that clearing the grid reads as Complete rather than Sharp.
That fix was right and it did not go far enough — the rungs BETWEEN the ends
are still noise on a 23-point board.

Also logged: `crafty` is clued as *"What twelve hours over coals with no
thermometer actually requires"* — which asks for a noun (patience, craft) and
answers with an adjective. The clue is well written and does not point at its
own answer.

## Ease of use — 8

Measured on the production export at 375×812: no scrolling, letter tiles
68×68 (well past the 44pt touch minimum), smallest target 24px (exactly the
WCAG 2.5.8 floor). The teach is one line — "Six letters. Six words. All from
the wheel." — and the Enter control appears only once three letters are
selected, which is progressive disclosure doing real work rather than
decorating. Row chips state each answer's length without spoiling it.

Held off a 9 by one thing: there is **no first-run explainer at all** on a
cleared profile. The single teach line may genuinely be enough — that is
defensible Wardle-style restraint — but nothing in the code shows it was
decided rather than lost.

## Fun — 7 at review, 8 after the fix

The clue writing is the product. *"Ash from the burn barrel to the pit, all
night, by somebody's nephew"* is a real sentence about a real thing, and it is
what separates this from every anagram wheel. The loop is tight: read, find,
the tile lands, the row fills.

What held it at 7 was that the rank feedback — the loudest thing on the board,
top-left, updating on every word — was the part carrying least meaning (see
Logic). The game's best material is the clue, and the most prominent live
readout was the least trustworthy number. With the ladder on rows that readout
is now exactly what it says, so this rises to 8.

## Engagement — 6 at review, 8 after the fix

No account, no ads, no nagging: the Wardle-clean posture is intact and worth
protecting.

But the completion screen — the first success a new player ever sees — leads
with three stat tiles reading **23 / 0 / 0**. Two of the three are zeros, and
one of them ("Streak") is zero *by definition* on a first clear. The moment
designed to feel like an achievement spends two thirds of its space telling a
new player what they have not got yet.

The day-2 hook is one line, "Play tomorrow to start a streak", and the
next-step CTA ("Warm-up 2 →") is clear and well placed.

## The one fixed second — Engagement — DONE 2026-08-14

A stat now appears only once it can mean something. `completionStats` in
`src/lib/game.ts` decides; the sheet just renders what it returns.

- **Score** always — clearing takes six words, so it is never zero.
- **Bonus** once there is one. Zero bonus words is the default, not a result.
- **Streak** once it outlives a day. `1` is not a streak, it is today — the
  rail already drew this exact line ("Play tomorrow to start a streak") and
  the two were disagreeing.
- **Warm-up progress** fills a free slot, because it is the thing that IS true
  on a first clear. Added last, so it never displaces a real result.

Measured on the production export at 390×844, studio, by clearing warm-up 1:

```
first clear        2 cols   Score 23 · Warm-up 1/2      (165px each)
returning streak   3 cols   Score 23 · Streak 6 · Warm-up 1/2   (106px each)
```

No zero appears in either. **Engagement re-scores 8**, which makes the surface
score 8 — Ease of use is now the lowest at 8, and its own gap (nothing shows
the first-run explainer was decided rather than lost) is unchanged.

The harness that proved it is `scripts/capture-complete.mjs`, and it earned
its keep immediately: run without an isolated browser context per case, the
second case inherited the first's saved progress and opened an already-solved
board, so no sheet appeared at all. It measures the rendered `<dl>` rather
than eyeballing the screenshot, which is the only reason that showed up.

## The original finding

Logic was the lowest at review and is fixed, which makes **Engagement the
lowest dimension and the next thing to take**. It is also the cheapest: the
completion screen leads with `23 / 0 / 0`, and one of those zeros is zero by
definition on a first clear. Showing a stat that cannot be non-zero yet is the
same class of mistake the ladder was — a readout that cannot mean what it
appears to.

It was not taken at review, because what replaces it was a design call: bonus
and streak could be hidden until they have a value, or replaced on a first
clear by the thing that IS true ("Warm-up 1 of 2 done"). Both were taken —
hide what is empty, and fill the gap with warm-up progress.

## The one that was fixed first — DONE 2026-08-14

Option 3 below was taken. `RANKS` is gone; the ladder is `RANK_NAMES` indexed
by rows filled, and `rankFor`/`rankLadder` take `(rowsFilled, totalRows)`.

Replayed the same board afterwards, one word at a time:

```
cry     1/6 rows · 1 to Sharp        Solid
fat     2/6 rows · 1 to Clever       Sharp
fry     3/6 rows · 1 to Fluent       Clever
cart    4/6 rows · 1 to Wordsmith    Fluent
tray    5/6 rows · 1 to Complete     Wordsmith
crafty  6/6 rows · maxed             Complete
```

One rung per word, no leaps, and "1 to X" every time — because it is always
one. The rungs are also identical on every board now, so three rows is Clever
here and Clever tomorrow; they used to move with each puzzle's point ceiling
and could not be learned.

`Genius` was dropped: seven names for the seven states of a six-row board, and
on this ladder it would have meant "found five of six", which is a good board
and not genius. **Logic re-scores 8.** Two things still hold it under 9 — the
`crafty` clue asks for a noun and answers with an adjective, and the
completion screen still leads with two zeros (Engagement, below).

The original finding, for the record:

**Logic, and specifically the ladder.** It is the lowest dimension, it is
measurable, and it is upstream of Fun — the rank strip is prominent precisely
because it is supposed to mean something.

Options, none of them chosen here:

1. Score the base closer to the other rows so no single word is half the board.
   Changes scoring everywhere; needs its own review.
2. Fewer rungs on small boards — a 23-point warm-up does not need eight.
3. Rank on ROWS FILLED (0–6) rather than points. Six rows, six steps, one per
   answer, and a leap becomes impossible by construction.

3 is the cheapest and the most honest, and it matches what the strip already
says out loud: "fill the six rows".
