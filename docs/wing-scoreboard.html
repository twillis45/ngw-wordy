What the player board just changed

31 seats reviewed the seven-tile dial proposal. Two findings move the board above.

Four of thirty-one seats noticed the off-theme rows

The whole rebuild assumed players register that answer words aren't about the theme. They mostly don't — the clue was carrying it. 0.64/5 on-theme was a craft failure, not a player-facing defect, and the seats that noticed (crossword, NYT, two others) marked it against the clue writing rather than the mechanic. Vocabulary-first authoring already banks the value at zero mechanical cost. It does not justify spending the dial.

Seven tiles: BLOCKED on accessibility, Grandmother veto exercised

Tile midpoints fall at 15.6% against a 15% tap-hit radius — 0.6% of forgiveness, on a control the motor-limited seat depends on. The low-vision seat is out at 320px. And the prize-word moment fires less often, because seven-letter words are guessed from partials far less than six-letter ones — that is the game's best moment, reduced. Board's counter-proposal: if it ever ships, it ships as an opt-in wide-dial mode at ≥390px, never as the default and never on the daily.

One board claim checked and rejected

The bench argued the dial and the 51px centre control would overlap at seven tiles. Measured, it is backwards: pushing tiles outward moves them away from the centre, and clearance at a 320px phone improves from 12.6px to 20.9px. The block stands on the tap-margin and low-vision grounds, not this one.

Board count is not a price variable

No seat changed its number between 71 boards and 136. $14.99 one-time stands under both. Players are buying "never asked again" and the clue voice — not volume. That retires "more content" as a retention lever and promotes progress durability to the widest gap on the board.
