# Foodways — Fish Fry, Cookout, Sunday Dinner, In the Kitchen

Cluster covers four packs in `data/themes.json`: `fishfry` (20 boards),
`cookout` (20), `sunday` (20), `kitchen` (20) — 80 boards, 480 clues. Standard
is `docs/research/README.md`.

Headline: **two clues are factually wrong** (`cookout` / `drinks` / `risk` —
Chicago lakefront parking does not work that way; `fishfry` / `whales` / `wash` —
Baltimore lake trout is not a lodge-basement fish). Every regional cookout
assignment survives. The three language flags the authors raised come back
split: **"front eye" is real but thinly documented**, **"icebox" is generational
rather than Southern or Black-specific**, and **the cast-iron-and-soap rule turns
out not to appear in these packs at all** — the two cast-iron clues that do exist
state the part of the folk method that modern test kitchens endorse.

## VERIFIED

### Baltimore lake trout is whiting, and the packs never call it a trout

The most-feared claim checks out on the biology. The Baltimore Sun identifies
the fish by scientific name as *Merluccius bilinearis* — silver hake, sold
commercially as **whiting**. It is not a trout and Maryland has no natural
lakes; Faidley's Bill Devine's standing theory is a corruption of "late trout."
It is breaded in cornmeal or cracker meal, fried hard, served on plain white
bread that the fillet overhangs on every side, and doused with vinegary hot
sauce.

- Baltimore Sun, "You asked: What is lake trout?":
  https://www.baltimoresun.com/2019/04/30/you-asked-what-is-lake-trout-the-story-behind-the-baltimore-delicacy-with-a-misleading-name/
- Baltimore City Paper on the carryout form:
  https://www.citypaper.com/bcp-cms-1-1282027-migrated-story-cp-20120307-featu-20120307-story.html
- Baltimore Food Chronicle on The Roost (opened 1974, owner Doris Williams):
  https://baltimorefoodchronicle.com/2013/10/03/the-roost/

The **venue** is where the packs go wrong — see WRONG, below.

### Whiting is the sourced fish of the Black fish fry

Whiting is specifically documented, not a guess. Whetstone traces the Nation of
Islam "cornering the market on fried whiting" in the mid-to-late 1970s, with
Baptist and Pentecostal revival tradition keeping catfish alongside it. And the
single most public fish fry in American politics serves exactly the form the
packs describe: **fried whiting on white bread**, thousands of pounds of it, at
Rep. Jim Clyburn's World Famous Fish Fry (first held 1992, 21–22 presidential
candidates attending in 2019).

- Whetstone, "Often Hidden, Fried Fish Is a Valuable Fixture in Black American
  Cuisine": https://www.whetstonemagazine.com/journal/often-hidden-fried-fish-is-a-valuable-fixture-in-black-american-cuisine
- ABC News on the Clyburn fish fry menu and scale:
  https://abcnews.go.com/Politics/thousands-pounds-fish-thousands-voters-22-presidential-candidates/story?id=63835003

Grounds `fishfry` / `filets` / `filets`, `fishfry` / `pounds` / `pounds`,
`fishfry` / `ladies` / `sale`.

### All three institutional strands the packs assert are real

The pack asserts Black church fundraising, Catholic Lenten, and VFW/Legion/fire
company simultaneously. All three are documented, unevenly.

**Black church/community — best documented.** Post-Emancipation and Great
Migration origin, proceeds to building funds, and the fish fry as the respectable
alternative to a rent party. The civil-rights fundraising line runs through
Georgia Gilmore's Club from Nowhere, which sold fried fish, pound cake and sweet
potato pies out of salons, cab stands and churches to bankroll the Montgomery
Bus Boycott.

- Cuisine Noir: https://www.cuisinenoir.com/fish-fry-tradition-fellowship-black-community/
- NPR on Georgia Gilmore: https://www.npr.org/sections/thesalt/2018/01/15/577675950/meet-the-fearless-cook-who-secretly-fed-and-funded-the-civil-rights-movement

**Catholic Lenten — documented, with a Prohibition twist worth having.** Friday
meat abstinence plus German and Polish Catholic settlement; Wisconsin's fish fry
was reinforced during Prohibition when taverns needed food revenue, and survived
the 1960s narrowing of abstinence to Lent because it had already secularized.

- Wisconsin Historical Society: https://www.wisconsinhistory.org/Records/Article/CS3968
- Travel Wisconsin: https://www.travelwisconsin.com/stories/a-brief-history-of-the-wisconsin-fish-fry

**VFW / Legion / volunteer fire company — real but under-historicized.** It is
an observable circuit documented through local-news fish-fry guides rather than
scholarship. Fine to depict; do not claim a history for it.

### Milwaukee perch, rye bread, potato pancakes — verified, price slightly low

`fishfry` / `asking` puts PERCH $14, RYE BREAD, POTATO PANCAKES on the poster
board. Lake (yellow) perch is the heritage fish, rye and German-style potato
pancakes are standard plate components, and the parish-hall venue is right.
Milwaukee-area church plates in 2024 ran **$15–$17 adult, $5 child** (Holy
Family $16, Queen of Apostles $15, Polish Center $17), so $14 reads as a couple
of years stale rather than wrong.

- Milwaukee Journal Sentinel church fish-fry roundup:
  https://www.aol.com/church-other-nonprofit-fish-fries-110149284.html

**Caveat worth knowing:** cod shipped from the East Coast is at least as common
in Wisconsin today as perch. Perch is the prestige order, which is exactly why a
hand-lettered poster would lead with it.

### Eastern NC whole hog — burn barrel, oak, shovel

`cookout` / `briefs` / `fire` ("Oak, lit in a barrel at eleven at night, so the
coals can be shoveled under the hog by two") is a correct description of a
documented method. Rodney Scott's burn barrel is two welded 55-gallon drums; wood
goes in the top and coals are pulled from below and shoveled under the hog every
10–15 minutes through the night. Texas Monthly on the Skylight Inn: "Oak burns
down to coals in enormous fireplaces before being shoveled into the pits on
either side of the splayed hogs."

- Texas Monthly at Skylight Inn: https://www.texasmonthly.com/bbq/eastern-nc-bbq-at-skylight-inn/
- SFA, *Cut/Chop/Cook* and the 2003 Scott's Bar-B-Q interview:
  https://www.southernfoodways.org/film/cut-chop-cook/ ,
  https://www.southernfoodways.org/interview/scotts-bar-b-que-2003/
- NPR on the burn barrel: https://www.npr.org/sections/thesalt/2014/02/04/271526910/when-his-pit-burned-down-a-southern-bbq-master-took-hogs-on-tour

**One sharpening.** Commercial pits put hogs on in the *late afternoon* and serve
at lunch the next day. The pack's 11 p.m. fire for a next-evening meal is a long
cook (~16 hrs) but within the documented range, and it is the right shape for a
family yard rather than a restaurant.

### Central Texas post oak, brisket, Big Red

`cookout` / `avoids` is clean. Post oak is the local white oak of Central Texas
and Franklin's sole fuel; mesquite is South/West Texas and the border. Big Red
was invented in a **Waco** lab in 1937 by Grover C. Thomsen and R. H. Roark as
"Sun Tang Red Cream Soda," renamed in 1969, and Texas Highways calls it "Texas
barbecue's unofficial signature soda." Texas Monthly documents it as a Juneteenth
staple, which is worth knowing given the adjacent pack.

- https://www.masterclass.com/classes/aaron-franklin-teaches-texas-style-bbq/chapters/wood
- https://texashighways.com/food-drink/big-red-is-texas-barbecues-unofficial-signature-soda/
- https://www.texasmonthly.com/being-texan/texas-primer-big-red/

One note of honesty: "he will not burn mesquite and he will tell you exactly why"
is a **preference** claim, not a rule. Accurate in spirit; there is no ban.

### North Alabama white sauce — every element of the claim holds

Bob Gibson (1888–1972) founded Big Bob Gibson Bar-B-Q in **Decatur, Morgan
County, Alabama, in 1925**; the restaurant marked its centennial in 2025. The
Encyclopedia of Alabama confirms the method the pack depends on: he split the
chickens, smoked them over hickory about three hours, **then dunked them** in the
mayonnaise-vinegar-black-pepper sauce. Sauce at the end, never before — which is
exactly `cookout` / `amulet` / `meat`.

- https://encyclopediaofalabama.org/article/big-bob-gibson-bbq/
- https://bigbobgibson.com/blogs/news/history
- https://1819news.com/news/item/big-bob-gibsons-bbq-celebrates-100-years-in-decatur

### SoCal fire rings still exist, and first-come is right for this board

They survived the fight. SCAQMD adopted amended Rule 444 in July 2013 (100-ft
spacing within 700 ft of homes; no burning on no-burn days) after Newport Beach's
March 2012 vote to pull all 60 rings; the Coastal Commission staff recommended
denial and AB 1102 passed the Assembly 64-0. **Newport never removed them.**

`cookout` / `ashore` / `shore` ("you get down there at seven to hold a fire ring,
because by ten every one of them is taken") is accurate at Dockweiler (~40 pits,
no reservation system at all), Bolsa Chica (~200 rings), Balboa Pier and Corona
del Mar. It would be **wrong only at Huntington Beach city rings**, which are
reservation-only at $200/day. The board does not name a beach, so it stands.

- Rule 444 text: https://www.aqmd.gov/docs/default-source/rule-book/rule-iv/rule-444.pdf
- Coastal Commission staff report: https://documents.coastal.ca.gov/reports/2013/7/Th12a-7-2013.pdf
- Dockweiler, first-come only: https://beaches.lacounty.gov/dockweiler-beach-fire-pits/
- Huntington Beach reservations: https://www.huntingtonbeachca.gov/departments/parks___recreation/beach_information/fire_pit_reservations.php

Tri-tip is also genuinely Californian: popularized in Santa Maria in 1952 by
butcher Bob Schutz, and Santa Maria style was recognized by the California State
Senate in 2012. https://santamariavalley.com/blog/the-origins-of-santa-maria-tri-tip/

### Caribbean Flatbush — verified, with a nationality worth keeping straight

East Flatbush (~149,000 people, roughly 72–77% Black) is Brooklyn's Caribbean
center — Jamaican, Haitian, Guyanese, Trinidadian and Barbadian — with Haitian
Creole the most common non-English household language in Community District 17.
Peanut punch (blended peanut, condensed milk, **nutmeg**) is pan-Caribbean and
usually credited to Jamaica.

- https://punchdrink.com/articles/peanut-punch-caribbean-drink-guinness-rum/
- https://caribbeanpot.com/peanut-punch-for-two/
- https://datausa.io/profile/geo/nyc-brooklyn-community-district-17-east-flatbush-puma-ny

**Precision, not a correction:** *curry goat with rice and peas* is specifically
**Jamaican** — red kidney beans ("red peas") with coconut milk, scallion,
pimento, thyme and Scotch bonnet. Trinidad's pigeon-peas-and-rice dish is
**pelau**, a different plate. `cookout` / `peanut` currently reads as a Jamaican
table, which is coherent — just don't describe it as generically Caribbean.

- https://www.foodnetwork.com/recipes/food-network-kitchen/jamaican-rice-and-peas-19549684
- https://thisbagogirl.com/trinidad-chicken-pelau/

### The half-smoke is DC, and DC-only

Coarse half-beef/half-pork smoked sausage originally from Briggs and Co., a DC
meat-packing company; made iconic by Ben's Chili Bowl, opened 1958 on U Street
by Ben Ali, a Trinidadian immigrant. The DC tourism board calls it "DC's
signature dish." `cookout` / `alcove` / `oval` is safe.

- https://washington.org/visit-dc/dc-signature-half-smoke-all-you-need-to-know
- https://greatstreets.dc.gov/release/bens-chili-bowl-half-smokes-inspire-wholehearted-love-dc
- https://www.npr.org/2009/10/11/113719465/remembering-founder-of-iconic-d-c-restaurant

**Do not move this board to Philadelphia.** Nothing supports half-smokes there;
Philly's block-party grill item is the hot dog, and its sausage icons are the
Italian sausage sandwich and the cheesesteak.

### The cast-iron clues are correct — and the doctrine the author feared is absent

Searching all 480 clues for "soap" returns nothing in a cast-iron context. The
packs never state the no-soap rule. What they do state is the part that modern
sources endorse:

- `kitchen` / `citrus` / `rust` — "What the iron gets if you leave it wet, and
  why she dries it over the flame." **Correct.** Water on bare iron begins
  rusting almost immediately; heat-drying on the burner is the recommended step.
- `kitchen` / `mobile` / `oil` — "A film wiped onto the iron with a paper towel
  while the pan is still warm." **Correct**, and correctly thin.

Lodge's own care line is "Wash with warm water, dry promptly, and rub with oil —
that's it." https://www.lodgecastiron.com/pages/cast-iron-101-cast-iron-myths

For the record, since it will come up in review: **mild dish soap does not ruin
seasoning.** Seasoning is polymerized oil — cross-linked fatty acid chains bonded
to the metal, not a grease layer surfactants can lift. The rule was true once:
early-20th-century soap was fat saponified with **lye**, pH above 12, and Lodge
names that as the origin of the myth. America's Test Kitchen: soap "will not ruin
your pan."
https://www.americastestkitchen.com/articles/5037-is-it-ok-to-use-soap-on-cast-iron

The sharp finding is that the rule outlives its chemistry *through* institutions:
**Mississippi State Extension still publishes the prohibition** as fact —
"don't use soap or cleaning tools like steel wool that will remove the
seasoning." https://extension.msstate.edu/blogs/extension-for-real-life/how-care-for-cast-iron

So: the packs are safe as written, and if a future board ever wants the rule, the
honest version is a character stating it, not the game asserting it.

## WRONG

### `cookout` / `drinks` / `risk` — Chicago lakefront parking

> "Parking in that lot without the permit on the dash. The tickets come at noon
> like clockwork."

There is no dashboard permit to forget. Chicago lakefront lots are **paid
parking**: pay-and-display and ParkChicago meters (63rd Street Beach roughly $2
the first hour / $7 for four; Montrose about $2.50/hr), and the Park District has
installed **license-plate-reading gates** at lots beside ten beaches — plates are
scanned on entry and charges begin automatically after about 15 minutes, roughly
$4/hr to about $24/day. Enforcement is automated, not a noon ticket sweep.

- https://news.wttw.com/2021/03/24/alderman-calls-equity-metered-parking-along-lakefront
- https://www.cbsnews.com/chicago/news/montrose-beach-harbor-drive-parking-meters
- https://chi.streetsblog.org/2021/03/23/runners-association-even-with-meters-driving-to-montrose-will-still-be-cheaper-than-cta

**Drop-in replacement (105 chars):**

> What you take at the lakefront lot, where a camera reads your plate at the gate and bills you by the hour.

**Two more facts this board should be built on, not against:**

1. The Park District permit threshold is **50 people** — a Special Event Permit
   is required at 50 or more, plus a **$45 application fee**, and is required at
   *any* size for amplified sound, alcohol, tents or guaranteed space.
   https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/picnic-festival-permits
2. **You cannot legally grill on a Chicago beach, in Grant Park, in play lots or
   in nature preserves.** Charcoal and propane are allowed only in enclosed metal
   containers in the ~120 designated grilling parks, coals dumped in the red
   receptacles, fines to $500. The board's "the lake is right there" is fine; a
   grill *on the sand* would not be.
   https://www.chicagoparkdistrict.com/park-policies ,
   https://assets.chicagoparkdistrict.com/s3fs-public/documents/grilling%20areas/Chicago%20Park%20District%20Approved%20Grilling%20Areas.pdf

### `fishfry` / `whales` / `wash` — lake trout in a lodge basement

> "What the paper towels never quite do to your hands after breading forty pounds
> of lake trout."

The fish is right; the building is wrong. Baltimore lake trout is **carryout
culture** — corner seafood carryouts, paper bags, aluminum foil, a fillet
overhanging white bread with hot sauce. It is not a fraternal-lodge or
church-basement fundraiser fish, and this board is unambiguously a lodge (the
adjacent `laws` clue is about the liquor board and the `heals` clue is about a
member's light bill). Naming it "lake trout" inside a lodge scene puts a
Baltimore carryout word in a room that has never used it — which is precisely the
tell the author was worried about.

Sources as under VERIFIED, above (Baltimore Sun; Baltimore City Paper).

**Drop-in replacement (89 chars):**

> What the paper towels never quite do to your hands after breading forty pounds of whiting.

This keeps the fish, the volume and the joke, and uses the name the rest of the
country actually says. Related: `whales` / `slaw` ("Between the two slices of
white bread, and if you say no she puts it on anyway") is fine for a lodge, but
should **not** be read as Baltimore — the Baltimore sandwich is white bread and
hot sauce, no slaw.

**If a future board wants Baltimore properly**, build it as a carryout, not a
hall: plexiglass window, foil, the fish wider than the bread, hot sauce not
optional. That is a board this pack does not have and probably should.

## UNVERIFIABLE

### Croaker as a fish-fry fish

`fishfry` / `filets` / `filets` — "Whiting, cut thin, three to a box, because
croaker takes too long to cook." Whiting is well sourced (above). **Croaker is
not.** Searches across food-history writing, Southern foodways material and
fish-fry journalism turned up whiting and catfish as the documented Black
fish-fry pair and no comparable attestation for croaker as a named staple. It
may well be regional lived practice — it is exactly the kind of detail only a
community reader will settle — but it currently rests on nothing.

**Optional drop-in if the pack would rather stand on sourced ground (89 chars):**

> Whiting, cut thin, three to a box, because the thick catfish fillets take too long to cook.

### "By the box" and "by the pound" as church-fundraiser units

The packs sell fish by the **box** almost everywhere (`filets`, `carton`,
`plated`, `aprons`) and by the pound in `fishfry` / `pounds`. The documented
Black-church fundraising unit is the **plate sale** — "fish plate," proceeds to
the building fund. By-the-pound is documented at political scale (Clyburn:
thousands of pounds), not at parish scale. No source contradicts "box"; there is
simply no source *for* it. The packs already use "plate" in `plated` and
`priced`, so the vocabulary is mixed rather than wrong. Flagging so nobody later
mistakes it for a checked detail.

### New England fire-department fish fries

`fishfry` / `flames` puts a haddock fry in a fire company bay. Haddock is
verified for New England; **fire-hall fries are documented in Pittsburgh and
Ohio, not New England.** Searches for New England volunteer-fire fish fries
returned nothing of record. The board may be a Pittsburgh practice wearing a New
England fish.

### Sidewalk charcoal chimney on a rowhouse block

`cookout` / `alcove` / `coal` — "Lit in a chimney of newspaper on the front steps
at noon, ash blowing down the whole block." No source documents stoop or sidewalk
grilling as a named DC or Philadelphia folkway. What *is* documented is adjacent
and supportive: the rowhouse stoop as the primary social space (Library of
Congress; AIA Philadelphia), and grilling at permitted block parties in the
closed street. Both cities run real permit systems — Philadelphia $25 for a
weekend party filed 21+ days ahead ($60 after), via the Streets Department with
neighbor signatures and police district sign-off; DC's DDOT block party permit
needs 30 business days.

- https://www.phila.gov/services/streets-sidewalks-alleys/street-closures/apply-to-hold-a-block-party-or-street-event/
- https://ddot.dc.gov/page/neighborhood-block-parties
- https://www.loc.gov/item/2019688760/

Plausible and culturally coherent; treat as invention, not citation. Note the
grill would be in the closed street under a permit, not on an open sidewalk.

### "Front eye" — real, but the citation is thin

`kitchen` / `fruits` / `first` — "The pan on the front eye is hers." The usage is
real and is **not** an error, but the dictionaries of record are paywalled and I
could not close the citation to the standard this directory sets.

What is verifiable: Wiktionary carries *eye* sense 17, "(US) A burner on a
kitchen stove, hob, or cooktop" (https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/eye).
**Dictionary.com does not have the sense at all** (checked directly). DARE's
public map index lists **"eye(s)"** meaning *a circular metal lid on a
wood-burning stove* — the wood-stove-lid ancestor of the burner sense — and the
1965–70 questionnaire item is F10, but the full entry is subscription-gated
(https://www.daredictionary.com/page/mapsindex/ ,
https://dare.wisc.edu/surveys/the-dare-questionnaire-1965-1970/). The University
of South Carolina's public Appalachian English glossary has **no** entry
(checked: https://appalachian-english.library.sc.edu/dictionary.html).

Geography and vitality, from community and trade sources rather than scholarship:
consistently South / South Midland / Appalachian, transferred from the removable
wood-stove lid to the gas or electric burner, and living — appliance-repair firms
treat it as current customer vocabulary
(https://homealliance.com/faq/why-is-a-stove-burner-called-an-eye ,
https://www.hillbillyslang.com/definitions/stove-eye/).

**Not found:** a single published memoir or food-writing instance of "front eye"
or "back eye." The front/back modifier is amply attested in speech and nowhere I
could cite. **Recommendation: keep the clue.** It is the right register and it is
not an error. Closing this properly needs a DARE subscription or a Google Books
full-text search.

### "Icebox" — living, but generational rather than Southern or Black

`kitchen` / `adults` / `dust` and `kitchen` / `bodily` / `idol` both use icebox
for the refrigerator. The best source on this is explicit that it is **"not so
much a regional thing as a generational thing"** and "fading fast" — *A Way with
Words* (Grant Barrett and Martha Barnette), whose caller example was St. Louis:
https://waywordradio.org/icebox-refrigerator/ . Dictionaries mark the appliance
sense dated (https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/icebox). DARE's
2013–14 survey did ask the question (Houses D10b) and icebox registered as a live
minority answer, but the results page would not yield counts:
https://dare.wisc.edu/surveys/survey-results/2013-2014/houses/d10b/

**No dialect survey or oral history isolates icebox as a specifically Black
Southern retention.** The packs do not claim that it is one — both clues just put
the word in an older woman's kitchen, which the generational evidence fully
supports. Keep.

The genuinely living half is culinary: **icebox pie and icebox cake** are current,
unmarked recipe names, strongest in Southern food writing, and Garden & Gun
publishes them as live categories
(https://gardenandgun.com/recipe/a-southern-summer-dessert-lemon-icebox-pie/).
*Our State* notes the word was filed under archaic everywhere except mid-century
church and Junior League cookbooks — which is the exact shelf these kitchens
would have. https://www.ourstate.com/icebox-cake/

## TONE — NEEDS A HUMAN

1. **Whether a lodge fish fry and a church-basement fish fry can share a pack.**
   `fishfry` runs Black church basements (`ladies`, `aprons`, `parish`), a
   fraternal lodge (`whales`), a Legion post (`legion`), a fire company
   (`flames`), a township hall (`hamlet`) and a ward political fundraiser
   (`measly`). Every one is real. Whether they read as one tradition or as a
   survey of other people's traditions is a judgement research cannot make.
2. **`fishfry` / `measly`.** The alderman working the line without buying a plate
   is a sharp, recognizable figure. Whether it lands as affectionate local
   knowledge or as contempt aimed at a Black political institution is a reader's
   call.
3. **Whether the `sunday` pack's authority figure is loving or oppressive.**
   Twenty boards of a woman who decides seating, when the meal ends, who gets the
   china and what may be discussed. The material is consistent and unsourceable
   as tone.
4. **`cookout` / `briefs` / `brie`.** The cousin who moved to Charlotte bringing
   a cheese board that sits untouched. Class humor about a specific person.
   Research has nothing to say about it.

## MATERIAL WE DON'T HAVE

Specific, sourced detail worth authoring toward.

- **The green ring.** Newport Beach fire rings are painted: **green = charcoal
  only**, unpainted = clean wood or charcoal. Balboa Pier has 31 rings (15
  wood-or-charcoal, 16 charcoal-only, 8 ADA); Corona del Mar has 24. That colour
  code is a direct artifact of the Rule 444 fight — a rule you can see from
  thirty feet. https://orangecoast.com/o-c-guides/where-to-have-a-beach-bonfire-in-orange-county/
- **A Baltimore carryout board.** See WRONG, above. The fish wider than the
  bread on every side is a better image than anything currently in the pack.
- **The Prohibition origin of the Wisconsin fish fry.** Taverns needed food
  revenue; the fry outlived both the ban and the 1960s abstinence change. A
  bar-in-front, fryers-in-back board has that history sitting under it unused.
  https://www.wisconsinhistory.org/Records/Article/CS3968
- **Georgia Gilmore's Club from Nowhere.** Fried fish, pound cake and sweet
  potato pie sold out of salons and cab stands to fund the Montgomery Bus
  Boycott, deliberately unattributed so sellers could not be fired. The
  `fishfry` pack has building funds and roof funds; it does not have this.
  https://www.npr.org/sections/thesalt/2018/01/15/577675950/meet-the-fearless-cook-who-secretly-fed-and-funded-the-civil-rights-movement
- **Mullet — "Biloxi bacon."** The actual Gulf *coast* fish, with Southern
  Foodways film documentation from Pensacola and town mullet festivals. Catfish
  is the *inland* Deep South and above all the Mississippi Delta, where 1960s–70s
  aquaculture made it cheap (Belzoni, MS bills itself Catfish Capital of the
  World). `fishfry` / `anchor` reads as a river hall and its catfish is
  defensible on those grounds — but if any future board wants the coast, the
  fish is mullet, not catfish.
  http://www.southernfoodways.org/film/mullet-fishing-in-pensacola/ ,
  https://www.themeateater.com/fish/saltwater/trash-fish-tuesday-striped-mullet-aka-biloxi-bacon ,
  https://visitmississippi.org/experiences/coast-to-delta-seafood-road-trip/
- **South Carolina mustard sauce and its German lineage.** `cookout` / `sauced`
  ("Yellow, not red. Anybody who tells you different is from another state") is
  **correct and better than it knows**: mustard sauce is native to the South
  Carolina **Midlands**, where the colony paid the passage of thousands of German
  families in the 1730s–50s along the Santee, Congaree, Broad and Saluda; the
  earliest documented recipe traces to the Holly Hill / rural Orangeburg County
  area in the early 1930s. Two cautions: the "not red" rival is **Lexington /
  Piedmont NC**, and eastern NC's sauce is thin, clear vinegar-pepper — so this
  clue must not be placed on an eastern NC whole-hog board, where "yellow, not
  red" would be a false opposition.
  https://www.piggiepark.com/how-mustard-got-into-south-carolina-bbq/ ,
  https://www.scbarbeque.com/bbq-history/
- **Minnesota walleye is the premium, not the parish plate.** `fishfry` /
  `hamlet` uses walleye only in a tall tale ("the year the walleye did not come"),
  which is exactly right — the everyday Lenten township-hall fish is cod or
  Alaskan pollock, and walleye clusters at restaurants and higher price points
  because much Minnesota walleye is Canadian-imported. Any future board must not
  put walleye on a $12 church plate.
  https://www.startribune.com/40-fish-fry-options-at-twin-cities-area-restaurants-churches/601571732 ,
  https://www.thecatholicspirit.com/nomeat/
- **Cast iron as inheritance.** Skillets carried north in the Great Migration and
  kept for generations, the dark seasoning read as an accumulated record of every
  fat that ever went in. `kitchen` / `foiled` / `life` and `kitchen` / `trails` /
  `last` gesture at this; there is sourced material to go further.
