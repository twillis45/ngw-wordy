# Current affairs — dated, decaying, quarantined from the catalogue

Everything in this directory has a date in its filename and a shelf life. That
is the whole reason it is not in `docs/research/` proper.

## The distinction that matters

`docs/research/` grounds claims that do not move. General Order No. 3 was issued
on 19 June 1865 and will not be issued on a different day next year. A citation
for it is good forever.

This directory holds material that is true *this month*. Who holds an office, what
a court has just ruled, what people are actually talking about. It is genuinely
useful — a pack written with no idea what is going on reads as embalmed — but it
is a different kind of fact and mixing the two would let a decaying claim inherit
the credibility of a permanent one.

## The rule for the shipped catalogue

**Topical material does not go into the 300 evergreen boards.** Those ship as the
paid catalogue and are meant to be as good in 2031 as today. A clue built on a
current news cycle is a clue that will be confusing within a year and wrong
within two, and the player has no way to tell a stale clue from a bad one — they
will read it as the game being broken.

If topical material is worth using, it belongs in a **dated, rotating pack** that
is explicitly of its moment, or in nothing at all. That is a product decision,
not a research one, and it has not been made yet.

## Sourcing

The standard in `../README.md` still applies: primary and archival first, then
journalism of record, then community and practitioner writing.

For discourse specifically — what is being discussed and how — the citation is
**reporting about the conversation**, from outlets with named authors and
editorial accountability. Not screenshots of individuals, and not compiled
activity of private people. A public figure acting publicly is a fair subject; a
private person whose post went viral is not a source to build a dossier on, and
this repo does not do that.

## Files

Named `YYYY-MM-<topic>.md`. Anything older than about a year should be read as a
historical snapshot of what was believed then, not as current.
