## 3. Voting rights — the law changed in April 2026

**This is the section most likely to be stated confidently and wrongly. Date every
claim.**

### Louisiana v. Callais — decided 29 April 2026

- **Vote 6–3.** Majority opinion by **Justice Alito**, joined by Roberts, Thomas,
  Gorsuch, Kavanaugh and Barrett. Thomas concurred, joined by Gorsuch. **Kagan
  dissented**, joined by Sotomayor and Jackson. Argued October 2025 and **re-argued**
  — a second oral argument was held in the autumn 2025 sitting.
- **Holding:** Louisiana's SB8 map, which created a second majority-Black
  congressional district in order to comply with Section 2 of the VRA, was an
  **unconstitutional racial gerrymander**; compliance with the VRA was held not to
  be a compelling state interest justifying that use of race.
- **What it changes:** reporting and the Congressional Research Service describe
  the Court as having **raised the bar for Section 2 vote-dilution claims toward a
  showing of intentional discrimination** — "evidence [that] supports a strong
  inference that the State intentionally drew its districts to afford minority
  voters less opportunity because of their race."

Sources: [Justia, Louisiana v. Callais, 608 U.S. ___ (2026)](https://supreme.justia.com/cases/federal/us/608/24-109/);
[Cornell LII, opinion text 24-109 (2026-04-29)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/24-109_2026-04-29);
[CRS Legal Sidebar LSB11431](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11431);
[SCOTUSblog case page](https://www.scotusblog.com/cases/louisiana-v-callais-2/);
[NAACP LDF case page](https://www.naacpldf.org/case-issue/louisiana-v-callais/);
[Stanford Law on the second argument, 4 Nov 2025](https://law.stanford.edu/2025/11/04/the-supreme-court-hears-second-set-of-oral-arguments-on-section-2-of-the-voting-rights-act-in-louisiana-v-callais/).

**Where sources disagree — say this out loud rather than picking a side.**
Advocacy organisations describe the ruling as having "eviscerated" Section 2
([Campaign Legal Center](https://campaignlegal.org/update/us-supreme-court-has-eviscerated-voting-rights-act-whats-next);
[League of Women Voters](https://www.lwv.org/blog/scotuss-final-blow-dismantling-voting-rights-act)).
The Congressional Research Service uses the narrower verb **"narrows."** Both are
describing the same opinion. Section 2 was **not formally repealed** — it remains
on the books and the Court did not strike it down. What changed is what a
plaintiff must prove. That distinction matters and is exactly the kind of thing a
clue would flatten.

**Unresolved as of 10 August 2026:** how far Callais reaches into existing
majority-minority districts nationally. CRS says existing Section 2-compliant
districts "might be challenged" as racial gerrymanders in light of Callais — that
is a forecast, not a holding. The downstream litigation has not run.

### Alabama — Allen v. Milligan, post-Callais

A fast-moving sequence, all in 2026:

1. **May 2026** — the Supreme Court vacated the three-judge court's 2023 ruling on
   Alabama's congressional map and remanded for reconsideration in light of Callais.
2. **26 May 2026** — on remand the trial court **reaffirmed** its finding that the
   2023 map was **intentionally discriminatory** and again preliminarily enjoined it.
3. **2 June 2026** — the Supreme Court **stayed** that injunction, restoring the
   legislature's 2023 plan. Alabama is therefore expected to run the **2026 general
   election under the 2023 map**, notwithstanding a live district-court finding of
   intentional discrimination.

Sources: [Supreme Court order, 25A1314 Allen v. Milligan, 2 Jun 2026 (PDF)](https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/25a1314_7m58.pdf);
[Loyola All About Redistricting — Milligan v. Allen](https://redistricting.lls.edu/case/milligan-v-allen/);
[Alabama Reflector, 22 May 2026](https://alabamareflector.com/2026/05/22/alabama-congressional-redistricting-case-heads-back-to-federal-court/);
[Alabama Reflector, 29 Apr 2026](https://alabamareflector.com/2026/04/29/callais-fallout-in-alabama-no-redistricting-now-says-ivey-partisan-divides-over-scotus-ruling/);
[National Redistricting Foundation, 26 May 2026](https://redistrictingfoundation.org/2026/05/26/nrf-applauds-court-for-blocking-alabamas-congressional-gerrymander/);
[Alabama Reporter, 12 May 2026](https://www.alreporter.com/2026/05/12/supreme-court-reshapes-alabama-redistricting-battle-again/).

Note the irony worth preserving accurately: **Allen v. Milligan (2023) was a win
for Section 2.** The 2026 posture reverses the practical effect of that win. A
clue or a summary written on the 2023 result is now misleading.

## 4. Live federal and state policy

### CROWN Act (hair discrimination)

- **Federal: not law.** The CROWN Act of 2025 (H.R.1638) was reintroduced in the
  House in **February 2025** and has not received a vote. Earlier versions passed
  the House in a prior Congress but were never enacted.
  Source: [Congress.gov H.R.1638](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/house-bill/1638/text).
- **States: roughly 30 have a version**, i.e. about 60% of states. Recent adopters
  named include Kentucky, Missouri, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island and
  Vermont. The exact count varies by source depending on whether local ordinances
  and partial (school-only or employment-only) statutes are counted — **do not
  print a precise number without checking which definition is in use.**
  Sources: [Economic Policy Institute](https://www.epi.org/blog/thirty-states-have-passed-a-version-of-the-crown-act-to-ban-hair-discrimination-continued-action-needed-more-than-ever-as-trump-administration-abandons-equity/);

  [Mercer roundup](https://www.mercer.com/en-us/insights/law-and-policy/roundup-us-employer-resources-on-hairstyle-nondiscrimination-laws/).

### DEI law and litigation — actively unsettled

- **26 March 2026** — Executive Order **14398**, "Addressing DEI Discrimination by
  Federal Contractors," requiring contractors to certify they do not engage in
  "racially discriminatory DEI activities."
- **February 2026** — the **Fourth Circuit** held that facial challenges to the
  earlier anti-DEI executive orders were unlikely to succeed, while expressly
  **preserving** the right to challenge specific agency enforcement actions. A
  narrow ruling that is widely being over-read in both directions.
- **April 2026** — higher-education and minority trade associations sued over EO 14398.
- **10 June 2026** — **19 states and the District of Columbia** sued in the District
  of Maryland to halt EO 14398.
- **July 2026** — a federal court in Washington blocked enforcement of certain grant
  conditions against local-government plaintiffs.

**Status: fluid. There is no settled answer to "is DEI legal" and any clue implying
one is wrong.**
Sources: [Dechert, Apr 2026](https://www.dechert.com/knowledge/onpoint/2026/4/federal-contractors-face-escalating-enforcement-with-new-executi.html);
[Ogletree on the Fourth Circuit](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/fourth-circuits-narrow-ruling-on-anti-dei-executive-orders-leaves-employers-with-broad-questions/);

## 6. Contradictions with shipped puzzles (`data/themes.json`)

I read every clue in the `hbcu`, `sunday`, `church` and `homecoming` packs against
the material above.

**Finding: no contradictions. Zero.** This is a real result, not a shrug, and it is
worth understanding why, because it is the packs' main structural strength.

The HBCU pack (20 boards) makes **no institutional claim that a 2026 fact could
falsify**. Its clues are about lived campus texture — the aid-office line, the
`denial`/`nailed` financial-aid letter in March, `chapel` attendance counted from
the back row, `earn` as twelve library hours applied to the bill, `binder`,
`bridge`, `bedpan` clinicals. There is no enrollment number, no funding figure, no
named institution, no claim about how many HBCUs exist or whether any are closing.
Nothing in the Saint Augustine's bankruptcy, the $500m/$435m funding shift, the
MacKenzie Scott gift or the enrollment trend touches a single shipped line.

The `sunday` (Sunday Dinner) and `church` packs are likewise about practice, not
institutions: the mothers' board, the usher board, the Hammond organ, the
benevolence fund, `anthem`'s "Lift Every Voice." The only borderline case is
`hbcu`/`campus`/`scam` — "the table by the union with the free t-shirt and the
credit form," which describes campus credit-card marketing. The **CARD Act of 2009**
restricted that practice; the clue is a period-accurate memory rather than a claim
about today's yard. That is a **tone/currency question, not a factual error**, and
under `docs/CULTURAL_BOARD.md` it belongs to a human reader, not to me.

Three things to flag **forward**, none of which are corrections:

1. **The `hbcu` and `homecoming` packs are exactly the model this directory wants.**
   They pass an August-2026 news check untouched because they were authored at the
   level of practice rather than policy. Worth saying explicitly in `CANON.md` as a
   design principle, since it is currently only implicit.
2. **Do not "freshen" these packs with anything in this file.** The rule in
   `current/README.md` is unambiguous and this material is exactly what it exists to
   keep out. Callais will be a footnote by 2031; the walk from the far lot in August
   will not be.
3. **If a dated rotating pack is ever built**, §3 is its spine, and it will need
   re-verification before ship — most of it has a shelf life measured in months, and
   the Alabama posture could change before November 2026.
