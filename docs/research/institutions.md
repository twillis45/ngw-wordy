# Institutions — HBCU, Homecoming, Church

Cluster covers three packs in `data/themes.json`: `hbcu` (20 boards),
`homecoming` (21 boards), `church` (21 boards). Standard is
`docs/research/README.md`.

Headline: **one board is factually broken** (`homecoming` / `jesuit` — Xavier of
Louisiana is not a Jesuit school and none of its numbers are right). Everything
else in the priority list survives, and two of the author's own flags — the
"invented" barn shift and the unleavened Communion bread — turn out to be
correct.

---

## VERIFIED

### The gender ratio — the premise holds, and it was understated

The packs were authored on "roughly 60%+ women." The real figure is higher.
NCES data for fall 2018 puts HBCU enrollment at 223,163 total: 81,055 men and
142,108 women — **63.7% women, a 1 : 1.75 ratio**. Women have outnumbered men at
HBCUs **every single year since 1976**, the first year NCES broke it out.

- NCES, *Historically Black Colleges and Universities: 1976 to 1994* (baseline
  series): https://nces.ed.gov/pubs/96902.pdf
- Series continued to 2020: https://www.statista.com/statistics/1118589/enrollment-historically-black-college-universities-enrollment-gender-us/
- Contextual comparison — women were 58% of *all* US undergraduates in fall
  2021, so the HBCU skew is real but not unique in kind, only in degree:
  https://nces.ed.gov/programs/coe/indicator/cha/college-enrollment-rates-by-gender

**Implication for the packs:** every authoring decision that leaned on a
woman-majority campus is safe, and if anything the packs under-committed. The
`hbcu` boards that centre women — `stance` ("how she holds herself at the front
of the line"), `binder`, `dancer`, `bedpan` (nursing clinicals), `agency` — are
demographically well founded, not decorative.

### 1890 land-grant HBCUs, and the barn shift is real

The author flagged `hbcu` / `canter` / `canter` — "The riding program at the
land-grant school, and the 6 a.m. barn shift" — as possibly invented. **It is
not invented.**

There are **19** 1890 land-grant institutions (Second Morrill Act, 1890), each
of which operates a college of agriculture: Alabama A&M, Alcorn State, Central
State, Delaware State, FAMU, Fort Valley State, Kentucky State, Langston,
Lincoln (MO), NC A&T, Prairie View A&M, South Carolina State, Southern,
Tennessee State, Tuskegee, UAPB, UMES, Virginia State, West Virginia State.

- USDA NIFA, 1890 Land-grant Institutions Programs:
  https://www.nifa.usda.gov/grants/programs/1890-land-grant-institutions-programs
- Congressional Research Service, IF11847: https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/IF11847

Fort Valley State University — the only 1890 land-grant in the 26-member
University System of Georgia — runs an **Equine Center with a nine-stall barn,
an outdoor arena, a covered arena, and riding trails on and around campus**,
with a five-level student classification ladder in which advanced students teach
their peers.

- FVSU Equine Center: https://www.fvsu.edu/academics/equine-center
- FVSU College of Agriculture: https://ag.fvsu.edu/extension/equine-center

**Sharpening available:** the clue says "the land-grant school," which is vague.
A nine-stall barn is a specific, sourced image, and the classification ladder is
a better hook than the hour.

### Xavier is the only Catholic HBCU, and it does have a pharmacy school

Both of those claims in `homecoming` / `jesuit` are correct. Xavier is the sole
historically Black **and** Catholic university in the country, and its College
of Pharmacy grants the PharmD plus an MHS and MS, and is among the top producers
of Black pharmacists in the US. Xavier is also a top producer of Black medical
school graduates per AAMC.

- Xavier, Facts & Figures: https://www.xula.edu/about/factsandfigures/index.html
- Xavier, College of Pharmacy: https://www.xula.edu/collegeofpharmacy/

Everything *else* on that board is wrong — see WRONG, below.

### Homecoming as an institution

- Howard University played **Lincoln University in 1924** in what is documented
  as one of the very first HBCU homecomings, built by Howard alumni off the Ivy
  League model. The Howard–Lincoln football rivalry itself dates to a
  Thanksgiving Day game in **1893**. https://thedig.howard.edu/all-stories/first-howard-university-homecoming
- The Smithsonian's NMAAHC frames HBCU homecoming as a Jim Crow–era safe
  gathering space as much as a football weekend — the alumni-return framing the
  `homecoming` pack uses throughout is the historically correct one:
  https://nmaahc.si.edu/explore/moments/homecoming-celebration-hbcus-and-their-legacies

This vindicates the pack's central move: `gather`, `comers`, `theirs`, `barely`
and `core` all treat homecoming as an alumni institution rather than a game.
`barely` / `barely` — "What anybody under the canopy could tell you about the
third quarter" — is the thesis of the whole pack and it is defensible.

### Divine Nine vocabulary

- **Probate / new member presentation.** Public presentation became *the*
  standard reveal after 1989, when BGLOs ended the above-ground pledge process
  following a death at Morehouse. Before 1989, new lines marched campus openly
  for weeks; after, the probate show is the first time a campus sees them.
  https://www.bet.com/article/hhndaz/how-probate-shows-became-the-pinnacle-of-black-greek-life
- **Stroll** — synchronized line dance, org-specific choreography tied to a
  specific song, led by a stroll master from the front; distinct from stepping.
  Univ. of Michigan FSL, History of Stepping and Strolling:
  https://fsl.umich.edu/article/history-stepping-and-strolling
- **Yard show** — the outdoor step/stroll showcase, homecoming and probate
  season. Same source.
- **Line sisters** — used correctly in `homecoming` / `courts` / `cost` ("her
  line sisters split it"). A "line" is one intake class; the term is standard.

`hbcu` / `pasted` / `step` — "Yard show footwork, learned in a basement at 2
a.m. with the lights low" — is exactly right, including the secrecy, which is
the post-1989 condition.

### Church — the usher board is a formal office with a trained signal system

This is the strongest verified material in the whole cluster.

- **White gloves** signal purity and service and are standard uniform for the
  usher board; the tradition traces to Catholic and Masonic "clean hands"
  practice. The frequently repeated slavery-origin story for the gloves is
  **not** documented and the most careful source available is openly sceptical
  of it — do not author toward it.
  http://pancocojams.blogspot.com/2021/01/why-black-african-american-ushers-wear.html
- **Hand signals are real, taught, and numerous** — ushers are trained in as
  many as **23 silent signals** so they can direct a room without interrupting
  service. Documented examples: a **loose fist at the small of the back is the
  "service position," meaning the usher is on duty** (George T. Grier,
  *Universal Church Ushers Manual*, 1948); **two fingers raised on the right
  hand requests an offering envelope**; **a right hand to the throat or necktie
  means "pay attention."**
  http://pancocojams.blogspot.com/2021/01/the-history-and-purposes-of-african.html
- **The board as an office**, not a volunteer rota: African American Lectionary,
  Ushers' Day cultural resources —
  http://www.theafricanamericanlectionary.org/PDF/Ushersday_CR_July1308.pdf

This verifies, precisely: `gloves` / `gloves`, `gloves` / `glove` ("One hand up,
one hand folded... means she is working, not visiting" — that folded hand is the
documented service position), `gloves` / `solve` ("in under a minute, without
speaking"), `rushed` / `usher`, `praise` / `pairs`, `bronze` / `zone`.

### Church mothers as a real office

Not an honorific. Cheryl Townsend Gilkes (Colby, sociologist and ordained
Baptist minister) is the scholar of record: *If It Wasn't for the Women: Black
Women's Experience and Womanist Culture in Church and Community* (Orbis, 2001)
documents church mothers as an institutional role in the Sanctified Church and
as the "foundation and stay" of the congregation, with community-mothering
practice as a base for political organizing.

- https://www.amazon.com/If-Wasnt-Women-Experience-Community/dp/1570753435 (bibliographic)
- Gilkes profile, Harvard WSRP: https://wsrp.hds.harvard.edu/people/cheryl-townsend-gilkes
- Deborah Crumbley, "Miraculous Mothers, Empowered Sons, Dutiful Daughters: Two
  Generations of Gender Practices in a Storefront Sanctified Church,"
  *Anthropology and Humanism* 32(1), 2007 —
  https://anthrosource.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1525/ahu.2007.32.1.30

The pack's dignity premise stands. `remain` / `remain`, `bolder` / `older`,
`bolder` / `role`, `bolder` / `lore`, `fasten` / `seat`, `maiden` / `maiden` are
all resting on documented ground.

### The funeral-home fan

Verified in every particular the pack uses.

- Roughly **8 × 8 inches**, stapled to a wooden handle, image on the front
  (Black families and children, Jesus, or Dr. King), and the **local funeral
  home's name, address and telephone number on the back**.
- Funeral homes bought that back panel as advertising. Under Jim Crow, Black
  funeral homes were among the few Black-owned businesses with capital, and the
  fans were both cooling and a circulation of Black dollars inside the
  community.
- Tennessee State Museum, Curator's Corner — Church Fans:
  https://tnmuseum.org/Stories/posts/curators-corner-preserving-african-american-history-and-material-culture-church-fans
- UNC, North Carolina Collection, African-American hand fans:
  https://blogs.lib.unc.edu/ncm/2019/11/18/new-in-the-collection-african-american-hand-fans/

`church` / `famine` / `fan` — "Cardboard, wooden handle, the funeral home's name
on the back, working harder than the window unit" — is correct down to the
material.

### Armor-bearer

Real role, correctly described, and worth knowing it is contested.

- The role as practiced (carrying the Bible, water, towel; a ministry of prayer,
  watchfulness and intercession; walking a step behind the leader) was codified
  and popularized by Terry Nance, *God's Armor Bearer* (1990):
  https://www.godsarmorbearer.com/books/gods-armor-bearer-volumes-1-2
- It is **not** a New Testament church office, and that is argued forcefully
  from inside the Black Baptist tradition — H.B. Charles Jr., "Armor Bearer is
  NOT a Biblical Church Office!":
  https://hbcharlesjr.com/resource-library/articles/armor-bearer-is-not-a-biblical-church-office/

`church` / `maiden` / `aide` — "Armor-bearer is the word here: water, towel, and
the Bible carried a half-step behind" — is accurate as practice. `church` /
`losing` / `lingo`, which lists armor-bearer alongside tarry service and A/B
selection as jargon nobody explains, is doing the right job.

### Women's Day is a dated, founded institution

`church` / `easily` / `easily` treats a three-hour Women's Day program as an
institution. It is one, with a founder and a date: **Nannie Helen Burroughs**
proposed it in her 1906 annual report as corresponding secretary of the Woman's
Convention, Auxiliary to the National Baptist Convention; the Convention adopted
it and set the **fourth Sunday in July** as National Women's Day, expressly to
fund foreign missions and to give women a platform to deliver researched
addresses. Burroughs held that post 48 years.

- The Union Review, "HERSTORY: Women's Day in the Baptist Church":
  https://www.unionreview.org/articles/herstory-womens-day-in-the-baptist-church/
- African American Lectionary, Women's Day cultural resources:
  http://www.theafricanamericanlectionary.org/mobile/PopupCulturalAid.asp?LRID=108
- Library of Congress on Burroughs: https://www.loc.gov/exhibitions/women-fight-for-the-vote/about-this-exhibition/more-to-the-movement/nannie-helen-burroughs/

### Unleavened Communion — the author's flag resolves in the author's favour

`church` / `safety` / `yeast` — "None of it in the Communion bread, because that
first meal was eaten by people in a hurry to leave."

Correct on both halves. Most Baptist and Black Protestant congregations use
unleavened elements, in practice a commercially bought wafer; the stated
reasoning is the Passover/Exodus 12 haste, and it is the standard argument
given, including the extension that the cup should likewise be "without leaven
or ferment," which is the theological route to grape juice rather than wine.

- GARBC Baptist Bulletin, "Unleavened Bread Required for Communion?":
  https://baptistbulletin.org/the-baptist-bulletin-magazine/unleavened-bread-required-for-communion/
- David R. Brumbelow, "Why We Don't Use Alcohol For the Lord's Supper":
  https://sbcvoices.com/why-we-dont-use-alcohol-for-the-lords-supper-by-david-r-brumbelow/

One nuance the clue is lucky to avoid: the wafer is *bought*, not baked, in most
churches, so "Communion bread" is doing gentle work. The clue does not claim
anyone bakes it. Leave it.

## WRONG

### `homecoming` / `jesuit` — the entire board rests on a false premise

> `jesuit` :: "Xavier in New Orleans. Fifteen hundred students, mass on Sunday, and a pharmacy school."
> `just` :: "How many the whole college enrolls: eight hundred, and the dean knows most of the names."

Three separate errors:

1. **Xavier University of Louisiana is not a Jesuit institution.** It was
   founded by **Saint Katharine Drexel and the Sisters of the Blessed
   Sacrament** — a women's religious congregation Drexel founded herself. It
   opened as a secondary school in 1915 and became a university in 1925. The
   Jesuit university in New Orleans is **Loyola University New Orleans**, which
   is not an HBCU. The board's base word and its base clue contradict each
   other.
   https://www.xula.edu/about/factsandfigures/index.html
2. **"Fifteen hundred students" is wrong.** Fall 2023: **3,181 total** — 2,586
   undergraduates in Arts & Sciences, 412 graduate students, 178 in the College
   of Pharmacy. Xavier's current published figures are 2,500+ undergraduate,
   200+ graduate, 300+ pharmacy.
3. **"Eight hundred" is wrong** by a factor of four, and it is inconsistent with
   the board's own other clue, which says 1,500.

Note also that Xavier is Catholic *institutionally*, not demographically — only
about **7%** of its students are Catholic and 79% are Black. A clue leaning on
"mass on Sunday" as a description of student life is overreaching even after the
numbers are fixed.

**Drop-in replacements** (24–120 chars, no answer or answer-prefix inside):

- `jesuit` → `New Orleans has a college of this order, and the Catholic school on the HBCU list is not it. Nuns founded that one.` (118)
- `jesuit` (alternate, shorter) → `Loyola downtown belongs to this order. The Catholic HBCU four miles away was founded by nuns.` (93)
- `just` → `Barely three thousand enrolled, and the pharmacy dean still knows most of the names.` (83)
- `just` (alternate) → `Not quite three thousand two hundred, which is small enough that the registrar recognises faces.` (96)

If the base word `jesuit` is retained, the board can no longer be about Xavier at
all, and `suite` / `suit` / `ties` / `site` would need re-pointing. **Recommend
re-basing the board.** `jesuit` is a weak base for this pack regardless — no
Jesuit institution is an HBCU, so the word cannot carry an HBCU homecoming
board honestly.

## The band-forward claim — the bench was half right, and half wrong

The bench's finding was that band-forward framing flattens 100+ institutions.
The *conclusion* is right; the *evidence offered for it* is not, and the packs
should not be edited on the version of it the bench stated.

**What is true:** three programmes carry the school's public identity, and are
what "HBCU band" means to a general audience —

- Southern University, the **Human Jukebox**
- Florida A&M, the **Marching 100**
- Jackson State, the **Sonic Boom of the South**

**What is not true:** that Howard, Morehouse and Hampton are not band schools.

- Howard's **"Showtime" Marching Band** has 150+ members, escorted Vice
  President Harris in the 2021 inaugural parade, and plays NFL dates.
  https://thedig.howard.edu/all-stories/how-howard-university-showtime-band-shaped-one-music-grads-journey
  · https://andscape.com/features/the-5th-quarter-howard-university-showtime-marching-band/
- Hampton's band is **The Marching Force**.
- Morehouse's is the **House of Funk**, with **Mahogany-N-Motion** (danceline)
  and **Maroon Mystique** (colorguard) — both composed of **Spelman** students.
  https://events.morehouse.edu/group/house_of_funk_marching_band ·
  https://www.mahoganynmotion.com/about
- Spelman has no marching band of its own, and that is because its students
  march with Morehouse — which is a *better* board than "Spelman has no band."
- The "showtime" style itself — precision marching plus dancing by the band
  members — dates to a **1947** shift away from corps style, so it is a
  tradition with a date, not a vibe.

**Correct framing for the packs:** the flattening risk is not that bands are
overused, it is that *one kind of band* is. `hbcu` currently has band material
in `campus`, `alumni` / `nail`, `danger` / `range`, `rained` / `rain`, `bridge`
/ `grid`; `homecoming` has `stride`, `gather` / `hear`, `alight` / `halt`,
`course` / `rouse`. That is proportionate. The single best existing line is
`homecoming` / `gather` / `hear` — "the small school's is thirty people" — which
does the un-flattening work the bench asked for, in one clue.

## UNVERIFIABLE

- **`church` / `safety` / `fast`** — "Twenty-one days every January, no meat."
  The 21-day January consecration fast with a daily scripture text is
  extraordinarily widespread across Black Pentecostal, Baptist and
  non-denominational congregations, and dozens of individual church pages
  document it. What could **not** be sourced is any COGIC *denominational*
  requirement of it — it is not in COGIC's official calendar the way Holy
  Convocation is. **The clue itself is fine** because it does not name a
  denomination; the pack's authoring note that this is "a COGIC fast" is what is
  unsupported. Searched: cogic.org, COGIC official manuals and convocation
  programmes, denominational calendars.
- **`church` / `salute` / `salute`** — "What the Men's Day program calls the
  part where the founders are named and the room stands." Men's Day is real and
  widely observed, but unlike Women's Day it has **no documented founder,
  founding date, or denominational origin**, and no source describes a standard
  segment called a salute. Searched: African American Lectionary, AME
  *Discipline* summaries, National Baptist Convention resources, COGIC. The
  asymmetry is itself the finding: Women's Day is a dated institution with a
  named founder, Men's Day appears to be a later local imitation of it.
- **AME Men's Day specifically** — nothing in the AME *Book of Discipline*
  surfaced establishing Men's Day as a connectional observance. If the pack's
  note attributes Men's Day to the AME church, that attribution is unsupported.
- **Baptist annual meeting and officer slate** — `church` / `salute` / `slate`
  ("The officers read out at the annual meeting, typed since October by the
  nominating chair"). Congregational polity means each Baptist church sets its
  own bylaws, so there is no denominational document to cite. The *structure*
  (annual church meeting, nominating committee, slate presented for
  ratification) is standard congregational practice, but it is bylaws-level and
  unsourceable from national sources. Not wrong; not citable.
- **The full 23-signal usher vocabulary.** The number 23 is documented and three
  individual signals are documented (service position, two fingers for an
  envelope, hand to the necktie for attention). No published complete list was
  found — the *Universal Church Ushers Manual* (Grier, 1948) is the primary
  source and is not online. **Do not invent a fourth signal.**

## MATERIAL WE DON'T HAVE

Specific, sourced, and currently unused across all three packs:

1. **Probate.** The single largest absence. It is the defining Divine Nine
   homecoming-season event, has a hard historical hinge (1989, end of
   above-ground pledging), and none of the 62 boards use the word. Neither
   "neophyte" nor "cross" appears either.
2. **The 1947 showtime shift.** A dated origin for the thing everyone pictures
   when they picture an HBCU band — precision marching *plus* the band members
   dancing — and it replaced corps style. A `hbcu` board could carry the year.
3. **Howard v. Lincoln, 1924** and the **1893** Thanksgiving rivalry that
   preceded it. The `homecoming` pack repeatedly invokes "the class of '74" and
   "the class of '89" but never touches the institution's own origin date.
4. **The 19 1890 land-grants and the funding gap.** The Century Foundation's
   *Nourishing the Nation While Starving* documents systematic state
   underfunding of the 1890s against their 1862 counterparts —
   https://tcf.org/content/report/nourishing-the-nation-while-starving-the-underfunding-of-black-land-grant-colleges-and-universities/
   The `hbcu` pack has budget-scarcity clues (`byline` / `line`, `amends` /
   `send`, `denial` / `denial`) that are all personal. None is institutional.
5. **The FVSU nine-stall barn and the five-level rider ladder.** More specific
   than the current `canter` clue and already sourced above.
6. **Nannie Helen Burroughs, 1906, fourth Sunday in July.** Women's Day has a
   founder and a date. The `church` pack uses Women's Day but not its history.
7. **The service position** — the loose fist at the small of the back, from the
   1948 ushers' manual. `gloves` / `glove` is already circling this; naming it
   is sharper than describing it.
8. **The nurses' unit in white.** Already used well in `church` / `remain` /
   `near` and `easily` / `lays`. Worth a board of its own — it is the least
   documented and most distinctive of the auxiliaries, and the pack currently
   spends two clues on it and no base.
9. **Xavier done correctly:** only Catholic HBCU, founded by Katharine Drexel
   and the Sisters of the Blessed Sacrament, ~3,200 students, top US producer of
   Black pharmacists and a leading feeder to Black medical school enrolment,
   ~7% Catholic student body. Every one of those is a better clue than the four
   currently on the `jesuit` board.
