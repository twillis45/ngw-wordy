Themed rows now relate to their theme — authoring method rebuilt

The owner's finding: "I don't like the answer words associated with each theme… not clever, just obscure." Measured, and correct — the 90s R&B pack averaged 0.64 of 5 rows that were music words at all. SLOWER → swore worse rose wore slow shipped under an R&B heading.

Root cause sat upstream of the clue writing: bases were selected for structural legality — letter count, answer count, anagram safety — and never scored for thematic yield, so the theme was applied afterwards as a label. The new pipeline in scripts/theme-yield.mjs inverts it: vocabulary first, boards fall out of it, pack size is an output rather than a target. Rows are tiered by recognition (acts beat titles beat craft words), plurals fold onto their stem, boards dedupe by idea-set rather than letter-set, and no row word may appear in more than three boards in a pack.

Shipped: 90s R&B replaced by The Nineties under a BLACK MUSIC category — 12 boards averaging 3.7 of 5 on-theme rows, up from 0.64. Catalogue 417 → 395: the 25 old boards and 9 donor boards deleted, each donor measured at 0–2 on-theme rows for its own theme before it was taken (NIGHTS scored 0/5 for HBCU). Five canon entries added, two stale ones repaired.

Two defects found in this work, both corrected: the yield scorer used a bitmask subset test, which ignores repeated letters — it counted cool, loot, free and total as spellable from six distinct letters, inflating supported boards from 45 to 118 and producing a "best board" that was illegal. And three drafted clues carried wrong dates, caught only because writing the canon entry forced the check: Monica's "The First Night" is 1998 and not her debut, Wu-Tang's "Protect Ya Neck" is December 1992, and "Gin and Juice" is a January 1994 single off a 1993 album.

Webster 1913 replaced with WordNet

The 1913 dictionary was archaic, carried the racial language of its era inside ordinary entries, and its transcription had unresolved provenance — three problems, one source.
