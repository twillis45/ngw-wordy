Ranks measure what the game asks for

The rules said "fill every row." Ranks measured every word the six letters can make — and the rows are worth a mean 27.4% of that. So clearing the grid, exactly as instructed, showed "Sharp": rank 3 of 8, with five ranks greyed out above it, gated on ~37 bonus words the player was never shown. Ranks now measure the grid; clearing it reads Complete. Extra words still score and still pay out in hints — their own honest track.

The interesting mode is on by default

Escalating mode — the search space grows as you clear rows, so row six is a different problem from row one — is the one structurally novel thing in the game, and it shipped off, buried in a sheet labelled "Both are off by default." The warm-up ladder still runs without it (a new player is learning one rule at a time), and it now explains itself in the coach line the moment a letter is actually locked.

Webster 1913 replaced with WordNet

The 1913 dictionary was archaic, carried the racial language of its era inside ordinary entries, and its transcription had unresolved provenance — three problems, one source. WordNet is modern, lexicographer-curated and permissively licensed. The obscure entries that produced the worst boards (obis, longe, dures) simply do not exist in it, so the quality filter is structural now rather than a blocklist to maintain.

Legal pages, no third party, CSP

Every word a player tapped was sent to a dictionary API with no terms, licence, privacy policy or logging statement — a GDPR transfer, a Families/COPPA failure and unlicensed content, all in one call. Deleting it buys a collects nothing, transmits nothing posture and lets CSP lock connect-src to self. /privacy, /terms and /support now exist — both stores hard-block without them. Service worker no longer caches error pages as the offline shell, and data files revalidate.

The 230 generic puzzles are a liability, not a catalogue

96% of a paying customer's experience is raw truncated 1913 Webster prose — "mise: the issue in a writ of right" — against the 4% that reads "mail: the thick envelope. You knew from the weight before you opened it." Two different products. Generated puzzles with dictionary definitions are a commodity at $0 in unlimited supply; the board was unanimous that adding more is "adding zeroes to a zero." Keep them as free practice. Never bill for them.

The free-hint line is blessed — do not touch it

assist.ts:69 gives a free letter to a player who stalls with zero tokens. Because the model is a one-time unlock rather than consumables, a stalled player is a churn event, not a revenue event — the incentive to monetize frustration is already structurally gone. The wall goes at the pack boundary: never mid-puzzle, never mid-hint, and the upsell fires on completion, not on a stall.

Price: $4.99 is validated — for the plan, not the product. At today's 10 themed puzzles it's a $1.99 app that ends before dinner. The honest risk: the writing does not scale and it is the entire product. The likely failure isn't a bad app — it's a good app with a tired second half. Decisions on record

  • Platform: web to 9–10 first. Stores deferred, not abandoned — the same work clears Apple's 4.2 bar later.
  • Money: free now; a single one-time $4.99 unlock once themed content is deep. The only SKU a backend-less app can honestly support — consumables don't restore.
  • Themes: deferred pending the eight questions.
  • Never ship: a streak-freeze or streak-repair SKU. The streak resets hard to 1, which is honest while nothing is sold and predatory the moment something is.
