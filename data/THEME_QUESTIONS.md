## 1. The hallway. Deliberate, or a hedge?

> **`icons`** (The Cookout) — *"Three framed in the hallway: Martin, Kennedy, and your grandmother."*

The canonical trio in that hallway is **MLK, JFK and Jesus** (Obama in newer
households). Swapping Jesus for "your grandmother" sands the church out of the
most church-shaped reference in Black domestic iconography — and church is the
thing this whole set is otherwise missing.

It's also the only clue in the file that works like a *reference checklist*
rather than a lived detail.

**If it was deliberate, what's the reasoning?** I won't rewrite your family's
hallway on a hunch.

## 2. Sitcom Sunday — Black canon, or a deliberate breather?

> *"Reruns, laugh tracks, and everybody's living room."*
> **`stoic`** — *"Unbothered on purpose. The father, every episode, same chair."*
> **`gals`** — *"What the announcer called them in 1994 and nobody blinked."*
> **`cost`** — *"What the very special episode was about, right before the hug."*

The seat reads these as **1970s–90s general American TV nostalgia**, not the TBS
Sunday *Martin* marathon. "The father, unbothered, same chair" is Archie Bunker;
James Evans, Carl Winslow and Philip Banks are loud, moralising and physically
present. "The very special episode" is a *Diff'rent Strokes* / *Blossom* device.

So the pack is shelved with four culturally-specific sets while not itself being
culturally specific — and nothing in the name or blurb tells a player which it is.

**This answer flips the verdict from BLOCK to SHIP.** If it's meant as a
breather, it just needs to say so. If it's meant as Black sitcom canon, it needs
re-authoring.

## 7. Who is the HBCU pack talking to?

> **`man`** (HBCU) — *"What your father called you the first time he meant it."*

Strong clue. But HBCUs are roughly 60%+ women, and across all 59 clues **no
woman has a role outside the kitchen, the receiving end of a bridge, or throwing
shade**:

> **`ample`** — *"More than enough. The amount she cooked for six people."*
> **`eat`** — *"What she says four times before anybody actually sits down."*
> **`plea`** — *"The bridge, on both knees, asking her to pick up the phone."*

Meanwhile `man`, `son`, `father`, `uncle` and the whole barbershop set carry the
interiority. That's a pattern, not one bad clue — the seat's clearest dignity
finding.

## Also worth a decision (not questions, just flags)

- **The Cookout has 1 puzzle** and sorts **last** in the Themes list, because
  `themeGroups` sorts alphabetically and "**The** Cookout" files under T — below
  "In the Kitchen". The flagship reads as an afterthought. Sort order is a
  one-line fix; depth is authoring.
- **`hades`** (Barbershop) — *"The Greek underworld, and the god who runs it,
  same name."* Dictionary filler in the most culturally specific puzzle in the
  file. It breaks the spell and should be replaced.
- **`scam`** sits inside **HBCU** — *"The table by the union with the free
  t-shirt and the credit form."* The predatory campus credit-card table is real
  and universal to all colleges, but the optics of that word inside that label
  are avoidable.
